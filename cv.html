Graduate Research Assistant2023–present

Texas A&M University · Surface Science Research Group

J. Mike Walker ’66 Department of Mechanical Engineering · PI: Hong Liang

Surface-engineered lubrication. Developed functionalized MoS2 with 30-day dispersion stability in water, EG and glycerol. Reduced wear by 93% in PAO and 81% in water versus base fluids, and 85% in lithium grease versus unmodified MoS2. DDA-CNPs achieved over 60-day PAO suspension stability and 26% lower friction at 1 N versus base oil.

Electrified interfaces. Investigated MoS2- and carbon-modified greases, achieving 93% lower wear with MoS2 at 40 °C and up to approximately 45% lower wear with CNPs, both at 3 A versus corresponding base greases. Distinguished bulk impedance from contact resistance; proposed an electrified-damage framework in an Editor’s Choice Perspective.

Electro-responsive fluids. Engineered CNPs@TiO2 suspensions with approximately 1,870% viscosity enhancement over the off-field baseline without voltage collapse. APTS–SiO2 achieved approximately 4.9 times the yield stress of bare silica, both at 3 wt.% and 3 kV/mm.

Thermal-fluid design. Combined 22 water–ethylene glycol–glycerol formulations and six DFT dimer models to develop a cohesion descriptor and identify a cooling–lubrication formulation window; manuscript submitted. Ongoing LAMMPS nonequilibrium MD benchmarks mixture thermal conductivity against experiments.
