Molecularly informed thermal-fluid design

Molecular interactions. Macroscopic transport.

Connecting liquid composition and molecular association with cooling and lubrication.

A 22-formulation water–ethylene glycol–glycerol study combines density, viscosity, thermal-conductivity and friction measurements with six DFT molecular-pair models. An OH-site-weighted cohesion descriptor provides a molecular interpretation of viscosity variation and the cooling–lubrication trade-off. The manuscript is submitted.

THE RESEARCH QUESTIONHow can coolant-lubricant formulations balance heat transport, flowability and interfacial protection?

What connects
the findings.

The pairwise descriptor is an interpretation of molecular cohesion, not a direct DFT prediction of bulk thermal conductivity or friction. Ongoing LAMMPS nonequilibrium MD investigates thermal transport in water–EG and water–glycerol mixtures. The active W–EG comparison uses rigid SPC/E water and experimental thermal conductivity as the benchmark.

Current simulation work includes density and volume equilibration, NVE energy-conservation checks, temperature-profile analysis and checkpointed production. Finite-temperature molecular-descriptor development is a separate ongoing extension.
